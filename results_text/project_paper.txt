Comprehensive Project Writeup

Title
Impact of G-Related Single-Nucleotide Variants on Predicted G-Quadruplex Motifs in Non-Coding Regions of COSMIC Cancer Genes

Abstract
G-quadruplex-forming sequences (QGRS/G4 motifs) are enriched in regulatory and untranslated regions and may influence transcriptional and post-transcriptional control. These mechanisms are especially relevant in cancer, where changes in promoter and UTR architecture can alter the expression of oncogenes, tumor suppressors, and other cancer-associated genes. This project developed a full computational pipeline to evaluate how G-related single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) affect predicted QGRS motifs in three non-coding region classes associated with COSMIC Cancer Gene Census genes: transcription start site (TSS) upstream 500 bp, 5'UTR, and 3'UTR. A cancer gene list derived from the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census was matched to representative transcripts using GENCODE annotation. Strand-aware FASTA sequences were extracted from GRCh38, scanned using a local QGRS implementation, overlapped with ClinVar variants, filtered to G-related SNVs, converted into mutant transcript-space FASTA sequences, rescanned with QGRS, and compared against wild-type outputs. Across all three region classes, G-related variants were associated with changes in maximum g-score, QGRS count, motif identity, and motif presence/absence. The strongest average negative effect on maximum g-score was observed in 5'UTR, whereas 3'UTR showed the largest total burden of variant-associated motif changes. These results support the idea that even single-base G-related substitutions may alter non-coding G4 potential in cancer-relevant genes and therefore may influence regulatory contexts important in oncogenesis.

Introduction
G-quadruplex structures arise from guanine-rich nucleic acid sequences capable of forming stacked tetrads. These structures have been implicated in transcriptional regulation, genome stability, RNA processing, translation control, and disease-associated mechanisms. Because G4 formation depends heavily on guanine content and spacing, sequence variants involving guanine are plausible drivers of motif gain, motif loss, or motif destabilization.

Cancer provides a particularly strong context for this question because gene dysregulation is a central feature of tumor biology. Regulatory changes that influence transcription, mRNA stability, or translation can contribute to abnormal expression of oncogenes, loss of tumor suppressor activity, or altered stress-response pathways. If G4-like motifs participate in these regulatory layers, then variants that reshape those motifs may contribute to cancer-relevant expression changes even when protein-coding sequence is unchanged.

Non-coding regions are especially relevant for this question. Promoter-proximal regions near the transcription start site (TSS), 5'UTRs, and 3'UTRs are enriched for regulatory signals. Variants in these regions can alter motif architecture without directly changing protein coding sequence. In COSMIC cancer genes, such changes may be especially interesting because they could affect regulatory behavior in genes already implicated in oncogenesis, tumor suppression, lineage control, DNA repair, or genome maintenance.

The goal of this project was to build a reproducible pipeline to test how G-related clinically observed SNVs change predicted QGRS motifs in non-coding regions of cancer genes. The project focused on genes drawn from the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census, which includes oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes, fusion-associated genes, and other genes with established cancer relevance. The project focused on three transcript-oriented region classes:
- TSS upstream 500 bp
- 5'UTR
- 3'UTR

Methods

1. Gene and transcript preparation
The cancer gene list was taken from the COSMIC-derived working file. A representative transcript was chosen for each gene using transcript annotations in GENCODE. The selection logic favored high-confidence transcript definitions, prioritizing MANE Select and canonical annotations with UTR support when available. Because the input genes came from the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census, every downstream sequence, variant, and motif analysis was anchored to a set of genes already implicated in cancer biology.

2. Region definition
Three region classes were defined for each selected transcript:
- TSS upstream 500 bp
- 5'UTR
- 3'UTR

The TSS region was extracted as a strand-aware promoter-like window. The UTRs were extracted in transcript orientation. For UTRs, exon-aware logic was required because UTRs are non-coding but still commonly exonic and can span multiple exons after splicing.

3. Strand-aware FASTA generation
Reference FASTA sequences were created for all selected genes and regions:
- TSS reference FASTA
- 5'UTR reference FASTA
- 3'UTR reference FASTA

For minus-strand genes, sequences were reverse-complemented into transcript orientation. For UTRs, exon blocks were spliced together before motif scanning.

4. Reference QGRS scanning
Each FASTA record was run through the QGRS scanner. Results were stored as:
- per-sequence summary
- per-motif hits

5. Variant overlap
QGRS hits were mapped back to genomic coordinates. TSS mapping used continuous genomic coordinates, while UTR mapping used exon-aware coordinate translation. ClinVar variants overlapping those QGRS regions were queried with bcftools.

6. G-related SNP filtering
Only G-related SNVs were retained:
- variants where the transcript-oriented change was to_G
- variants where the transcript-oriented change was from_G

This filter was applied in transcript-space rather than raw genomic allele space, which was essential for minus-strand genes.

7. Mutant FASTA generation
One mutant FASTA sequence was created per unique SNP event. SNPs were applied independently rather than combined. This means each mutant sequence differs from the reference by exactly one G-related SNV.

8. Mutant QGRS scanning
All mutant FASTA sequences were rescanned with QGRS. Mutant summary and motif-level outputs were stored for each region.

9. Wild vs mutant comparison
For each SNP-mutant sequence, wild-type and mutant QGRS outputs were compared. The comparison tracked:
- QGRS count
- maximum g-score
- maximum tetrad count
- top motif sequence
- motif gain/loss events

Why the regions may differ
- TSS
  - fixed 500 bp windows
  - promoter-proximal and relatively standardized in size
  - fewer total motif opportunities than longer transcript regions
- 5'UTR
  - shorter than most 3'UTRs, but often compact and structurally constrained
  - single-base changes may more easily destabilize a dominant motif
  - showed the strongest average drop in max g-score
- 3'UTR
  - longest region class and largest total motif burden
  - more opportunities for motif turnover and redistribution
  - many changes affected motif landscape without greatly shifting the top summary score

What g-score changes may mean
- Higher max g-score in the mutant may indicate that the variant strengthened the most favorable quadruplex-like motif in that region.
- Lower max g-score may indicate motif weakening or less favorable G-run/loop arrangement.
- No change in max g-score does not necessarily mean no biological effect.
- In several cases, the top motif sequence changed even when the top score stayed unchanged.
- Therefore, both summary-level metrics and motif-sequence identity matter.

What motif count changes may mean
- Fewer QGRS motifs may suggest reduced structural diversity or loss of candidate quadruplex sites.
- More QGRS motifs may suggest emergence of additional quadruplex-compatible arrangements.
- Complete loss cases, especially in 5'UTR, may indicate that a single SNV removed the only detectable motif in that region.

Limitations
- The modeled variants are SNVs only.
- Insertions and deletions were not included in the mutant FASTA or comparison pipeline.
- 5'UTR and 3'UTR reference scans used bounded execution to avoid extremely long runtime on very difficult sequences; a small number of reference UTR sequences reached timeout limits.
- QGRS detection is predictive and does not prove in vivo quadruplex formation.
- The analysis used one representative transcript per gene rather than all transcript isoforms.

Conclusion
This project demonstrates that G-related SNVs can alter predicted G-quadruplex potential across multiple classes of non-coding regions in COSMIC cancer genes. The strongest average effect on maximum g-score was observed in 5'UTR, while 3'UTR carried the largest total burden of G-related variant overlaps and motif changes. TSS showed a moderate but biologically meaningful set of effects, including both strengthening and weakening examples. Across all three region types, the results indicate that even when summary-level metrics remain unchanged, motif identity can still change substantially. Because the analyzed genes were selected from a cancer-focused gene census, these findings are particularly relevant to the idea that non-coding sequence variation may reshape regulatory G4 landscapes in oncogenes, tumor suppressors, and other genes with established roles in tumor biology.
