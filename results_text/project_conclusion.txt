Project Conclusion

This project built a full computational pipeline to evaluate how G-related single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) may affect G-quadruplex-forming sequences (QGRS/G4 motifs) in non-coding regulatory regions of COSMIC cancer genes. The analysis focused on three transcript-oriented regions: TSS upstream 500 bp, 5'UTR, and 3'UTR.

Main goal achieved
- The project successfully moved from gene list to reference sequence extraction, QGRS discovery, ClinVar overlap, G-related SNP filtering, mutant sequence generation, mutant QGRS scanning, and wild-vs-mutant comparison.
- This means the pipeline now captures not only where G4-like motifs occur, but also how they may change after clinically observed G-related variants are introduced into genes with known cancer relevance.

Key biological conclusion
- G-related SNVs can alter quadruplex potential in all three non-coding region classes.
- The observed changes include:
  - reduction in maximum g-score
  - increase in maximum g-score
  - reduction in the number of detected QGRS motifs
  - complete loss of detectable motifs in some cases
  - unchanged summary scores but altered top-motif sequence composition
- Because the analyzed genes came from the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census, these findings are relevant to cancer-associated regulatory regions rather than to a random gene background.

Overall findings by region

TSS
- Total ClinVar overlaps in TSS QGRS regions: 442
- G-related overlaps: 307
- Mean delta QGRS count after mutation: -0.081
- Mean delta max g-score after mutation: -1.41
- Interpretation:
  - TSS variants often preserved the general number of motifs, but many changed motif composition or weakened the strongest motif.
  - A smaller subset strengthened the top motif.
- In a cancer context, these promoter-proximal changes may matter because altered quadruplex potential near transcription start sites could influence transcriptional control of cancer genes.

5'UTR
- Total ClinVar overlaps in 5'UTR QGRS regions: 602
- G-related overlaps: 406
- Mean delta QGRS count after mutation: -0.113
- Mean delta max g-score after mutation: -3.377
- Interpretation:
  - 5'UTR showed the strongest average reduction in top motif strength among the three regions.
  - Some variants caused complete loss of detectable QGRS motifs.
  - This suggests the 5'UTR may be particularly sensitive to G-related substitutions in the context of quadruplex organization.
- In cancer genes, this raises the possibility that 5'UTR variants may influence translation-associated regulation or transcript handling in ways that affect oncogene or tumor suppressor output.

3'UTR
- Total ClinVar overlaps in 3'UTR QGRS regions: 974
- G-related overlaps: 638
- Mean delta QGRS count after mutation: -0.133
- Mean delta max g-score after mutation: -0.417
- Interpretation:
  - 3'UTR contained the largest number of QGRS-associated SNPs and affected genes.
  - The average impact on max g-score was milder than in 5'UTR, but the total motif landscape was broad and often restructured.
  - Many variants changed motif composition without strongly changing the top summary score.
- In cancer-relevant genes, this could matter for post-transcriptional regulation, RNA stability, localization, or protein-binding landscapes tied to the 3'UTR.

Strong representative examples
- TSS increase:
  - XPA, chr9:97697358, to_G, delta max g-score +19
- TSS decrease:
  - CD274, chr9:5450497, from_G, delta max g-score -22
- 5'UTR increase:
  - RB1, chr13:48303780, to_G, delta max g-score +10
- 5'UTR strong disruption:
  - MSH2, chr2:47403172, from_G, complete QGRS loss
- 3'UTR increase:
  - CDH1, chr16:68833515, to_G, delta max g-score +18
- 3'UTR decrease:
  - SMAD3, chr15:67191109, from_G, delta max g-score -27

Interpretation of region-specific differences
- TSS regions were fixed-size promoter-proximal windows and therefore more controlled in length.
- 5'UTRs were shorter than 3'UTRs on average but often highly structured and more sensitive to local motif disruption.
- 3'UTRs were the longest and richest in total QGRS opportunities, which likely explains why they produced the largest number of total variant overlaps and motif changes.
- The stronger average negative effect in 5'UTR may reflect the compact arrangement of G-rich motifs in those regions, where even a single base change can destabilize a motif more strongly.
- Because these regions belong to COSMIC cancer genes, differences across TSS, 5'UTR, and 3'UTR can be interpreted in the context of promoter control, translational regulation, and post-transcriptional regulation of cancer-associated transcripts.

Important methodological conclusion
- Strand-aware transcript-space handling was essential.
- Minus-strand variants had to be converted into transcript-oriented allele changes before mutant FASTA generation.
- Exon-aware handling for UTRs was also essential because 5'UTR and 3'UTR are spliced transcript regions, not always single continuous genomic blocks.

Important scope note
- The current analysis includes point mutations only.
- Specifically, it models G-related single-nucleotide changes:
  - to_G
  - from_G
- Insertions and deletions were not included in the mutant FASTA or comparison steps.

Overall conclusion
- The results support the central hypothesis that G-related variation can meaningfully alter predicted quadruplex potential in non-coding cancer-gene regions.
- These effects are region-dependent and can appear as:
  - stronger or weaker top motifs
  - motif loss or gain
  - motif sequence reorganization without obvious summary-level change
- The fact that the gene set was derived from the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census strengthens the biological relevance of these observations for cancer-focused regulatory analysis.
- The pipeline is now complete enough to support downstream biological interpretation, figure generation, and manuscript or poster presentation in a cancer-gene context.

Recommended next steps
- Build a combined cross-region summary figure and table for publication-style reporting.
- Rank variants by strongest decrease or increase in max g-score.
- Explore whether ClinVar significance categories are enriched among stronger QGRS-altering variants.
- Optionally extend the pipeline later to include insertions and deletions as a separate analysis.
